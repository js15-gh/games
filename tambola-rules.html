Tambola (Housie) Rules

Tambola — Housie, or Indian bingo — needs no skill at all, which is exactly why it works at a party where the players range from eight years old to eighty.

The ticket

A proper Tambola ticket is a strict thing, not just random numbers in a grid. Every ticket is three rows by nine columns and holds fifteen numbers, leaving twelve blanks.

  • Each row holds exactly five numbers.
  • Each column holds one to three numbers, never none.
  • Column one carries 1–9, column two 10–19, and so on; the last column carries 80–90.
  • Within a column, numbers increase downwards.

Those constraints are why hand-made tickets so often turn out invalid, and why an invalid ticket ruins a game — someone eventually cannot win a line they should have won.

How a game runs

One person calls numbers from 1 to 90 at random, without repeats. Players mark the called number if it appears on their ticket. When a player completes a prize they claim it out loud, and the claim is checked against the numbers called so far.

The standard prizes

  • Early Five — the first player to mark any five numbers.
  • Top Line — all five numbers in the first row.
  • Middle Line — all five in the middle row.
  • Bottom Line — all five in the last row.
  • Four Corners — the first and last number of the top and bottom rows.
  • Full House — every number on the ticket. The big one, and usually the last.

Hosts often add their own: Early Seven, Breakfast (first row's first three), Pyramid, and so on. Agree the list before you start, because adding a prize halfway is how arguments begin.

False claims

The traditional penalty is that a wrong claim disqualifies you from that prize. In practice somebody has to re-read the whole called list to check, which stops the game dead for several minutes and is nobody's idea of fun.

This is the part worth handing to a phone. If claims are checked automatically against your actual ticket and the numbers actually called, a wrong claim is simply refused, quietly, and the game keeps moving.

How many players

Tambola is one of the very few games that improves with a crowd. Six is fine; thirty is better. There is no turn order, so nobody waits.
