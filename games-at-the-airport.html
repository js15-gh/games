For the waiting

Games at the Airport

Delays are the one time a group has hours together, nothing to do, and no equipment. No cards, no board, no table — just a row of seats near a charging point and everyone's phone.

Why phones beat a deck of cards here

Nobody packs a card deck. Even if someone has one, airport seating gives you nowhere to put it and gate areas are too loud for anything needing careful explanation. What travels well is a game where each person looks at their own screen and talks to the people beside them.

The best ones for a departure lounge

Ito — quiet, absorbing, works in a row of seats

Everyone gets a secret number from 1 to 100 and a shared theme. Describe your number through the theme, then play in ascending order without saying the number. It needs no table and no shouting, which suits a gate area better than most party games.

Play Ito2–10 players · nothing to set up · 100 themes

Bollywood Jeopardy — for a long delay

Twenty-five clues, teams take turns, no buzzer race. Pick a decade and it fills the time properly — half an hour or more. Good when the group is settled and not going anywhere.

Play Bollywood JeopardyTeams · pick a decade · 30–45 min

Antakshari — if you're not in a quiet zone

Works anywhere a bit of humming won't annoy anyone. On a long layover it will comfortably eat an hour, and people can join in as they come back from wandering.

Play AntakshariTeams · the phone keeps the letter and the list

Practical notes for travelling

  • These are small pages, so they load on weak airport wifi and don't eat much data.
  • Everyone needs their own phone and the same link — there is no host device or screen.
  • Nothing to install, so nobody has to fight an app store on airport wifi.
  • Screens off, phones in pockets when boarding starts. Nothing is lost — the game is on a link, so you can pick it up on the other side.

Every game here is free, runs in a browser, and needs no app or sign-up. One person creates a game, shares the link, everyone plays on their own phone.
